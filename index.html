WAIT is a valid decision. A beginner does not need to trade every stock every day.
What LONG / SHORT / WAIT means
LONG — You are looking for the stock to rise. The engine wants several bullish factors to agree before suggesting this direction.
SHORT — You are looking for the stock to fall. Shorting can have losses greater than your initial sale proceeds if price rises sharply, so beginners should use extra caution.
WAIT — Evidence conflicts, data is weak, or the reward does not justify the risk. Doing nothing is often the correct trade decision.

5 — Where beginners can find trade ideas
1
Start with the market.
Check broad indexes such as SPY and QQQ. Long setups are easier when the market is rising; short setups are easier when the market is weakening.
2
Look for liquid, widely followed stocks.
Beginners usually benefit from tighter spreads and plenty of trading volume rather than obscure low-volume names.
3
Look for a catalyst.
Earnings, guidance, major company news, analyst changes, sector news or macro events can create movement. Verify the source and date.
4
Check trend + level + volume.
A stronger setup often has a clear trend, a nearby support/resistance level and enough volume to confirm participation.
5
Wait for confirmation.
A setup is not the same as an entry. Price should behave the way your plan expected before you commit capital.
6
Skip bad reward/risk.
If the stop has to be very far away or the target is too close, WAIT rather than forcing a trade.
